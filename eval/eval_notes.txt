== Photosynthesis ==
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy.
It occurs in the chloroplasts, specifically using the pigment chlorophyll. The overall reaction
takes carbon dioxide and water and, using sunlight, produces glucose and oxygen. There are two
main stages: the light-dependent reactions (which occur in the thylakoid membranes and produce
ATP and NADPH) and the Calvin cycle (the light-independent reactions in the stroma, which fix
carbon dioxide into glucose). Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green,
which is why plants look green.

== Cellular Respiration ==
Cellular respiration is how cells release energy stored in glucose to make ATP, the cell's energy
currency. It has three stages: glycolysis (in the cytoplasm, splitting glucose into two pyruvate
molecules), the Krebs cycle (in the mitochondrial matrix), and the electron transport chain (on the
inner mitochondrial membrane, where most ATP is produced). Aerobic respiration needs oxygen and
yields about 36-38 ATP per glucose; without oxygen, cells fall back on fermentation, which yields
far less. Respiration is, in a sense, the reverse of photosynthesis: it consumes oxygen and glucose
and releases carbon dioxide and water.

== Mitosis ==
Mitosis is the division of a single cell into two genetically identical daughter cells. Its phases,
in order, are prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase, followed by cytokinesis (the splitting
of the cytoplasm). During metaphase the chromosomes line up along the cell's equator; during
anaphase the sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles. Mitosis is used for growth and for
repairing tissue. It differs from meiosis, which produces four genetically varied cells with half
the chromosome number and is used to make gametes.

== Newton's Laws of Motion ==
Newton's first law (inertia) states that an object stays at rest, or keeps moving at constant
velocity, unless acted on by a net external force. Newton's second law states that the net force on
an object equals its mass times its acceleration: F = m * a. Newton's third law states that for
every action there is an equal and opposite reaction — forces always come in pairs. These three laws
form the foundation of classical mechanics.

== The Water Cycle ==
The water cycle describes how water moves around the Earth. Evaporation turns liquid water into
vapour using energy from the Sun; transpiration adds vapour released by plants. That vapour rises,
cools, and undergoes condensation to form clouds. Precipitation (rain, snow, sleet, or hail) then
returns water to the surface, where collection gathers it into rivers, lakes, and oceans, and the
cycle repeats.
